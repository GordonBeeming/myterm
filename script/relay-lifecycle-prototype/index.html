    Mechanics to decide

    Contact that counts

    Propose endpoint-authenticated, fresh challenge responses every 30 seconds, with a 120-second lease. Idle terminal traffic is irrelevant. Only a valid endpoint response renews contact; operator jobs and duplicate responses do not.

    A lost socket's inactivity clock starts at its last proven contact. The lease delays detection; it does not add two minutes to the 90-day limit.

    One ordered account history

    Serialize setting changes, enrollment, fresh connections, moves and closure. Every setting has a revision. Ignore stale revisions and duplicate opt-outs. The contact carrying an opt-out is processed before that opt-out starts its grace period.

    Propose that a committed deadline wins at equality: contact must commit before the deadline to cancel it. At the boundary, checking expiry and accepting contact are one serialized operation.

    Restore without time travel

    Propose a separate durable lifecycle journal containing activity high-water marks, setting revisions, grace cancellation, move generation and closure. Reconcile it before opening routes after a restore. Backups alone can lose a recent cancellation.

    Use the agreed non-restored deletion ledger. If current suppression evidence is unavailable, deny new relay access and closure acknowledgment until it returns.

    Boundaries and acceptance evidence

    Local verification: 21 reducer checks passed. Run node verify.cjs from this artifact folder to repeat them. Browser QA uses a 1920 × 842 viewport, capped by the display. Restore and restart actions preserve the in-memory journal by construction; real persistence and recovery still require separate integration evidence.

    This reducer models valid authentication as an input. It supplies no cryptography, sockets, durable writes, provider retention or real account cleanup. A malicious relay can suppress messages, falsify its clocks or refuse deletion; endpoint encryption cannot force honest timer enforcement.
